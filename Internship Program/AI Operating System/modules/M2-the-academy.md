## What this is

Two parts. The **induction** below gets a new Partner working the TractIn way in their first month. The **Knowledge Builder ladder** (already built, in the curriculum) takes them from there to leading a client build. This page is the induction and the doorway into the ladder. It routes you through the other modules in order rather than repeating them.

A new Partner should never wonder what to do next in their first month. This page is the answer.

---

## Before you start: which tier are you?

Three tiers, and you enter at the one your role needs (the full guide is in [M1](M1-the-stack.md)).

- **Floor:** every Partner, including non-technical. Most senior strategy Partners live here, and that is the spine of a build, not the support act. The parts that decide whether a build works (the problem, the questions, the adoption) are consulting, not code.
- **Build:** Partners who make the thing.
- **Architect:** the technical core, one or two to start.

You are never stuck. You climb when the work asks.

## Day 1: get set up, see the machine

- [ ] Set up your stack at your tier. Floor is about 15 minutes. ([M1](M1-the-stack.md))
- [ ] Read the map. The whole machine on one page. ([the OS map](../TractIn-AI-Operating-System.md))
- [ ] Read the firm values and the culture playbook. How we work, not just what we use. (`08. Leadership Office/TractIn AI Culture Playbook.md`)
- [ ] Find your badge: Knowledge Partner, Builder, or Architect. (`04. HR and Training/03. Training Sessions/AI-Academy-Knowledge-Builder-Curriculum.md`)
- [ ] Run your first `/quality-judge` and `/critique` on a paragraph, and `/council` on a real question. Quality Judge is the fast pre-gate; critique is the deeper read and stays mandatory for client-facing work.

By end of day one: your tools work, you have seen the machine, you know which badge you are working toward, and you have met the discipline that never switches off. We de-slop everything.

## Week 1: learn how we work

- [ ] Do the two foundations: F1 (Creating Rapid AI Prototypes, `04. HR and Training/03. Training Sessions/1. 26 May - AI builder training by JC/`) and F2 (Using AI Well, the TRUE, SOUND, TAILORED, OURS quality check, `.../2. 16 Jun - AI quality assurance/`).
- [ ] Read the Flywheel ([M3](M3-the-flywheel.md)). How a problem becomes a proven asset. Note which stages are yours: Tier 0 owns Curiosity, Challenge, and Prove; Builders own Prototype, Deliver, Embed.
- [ ] Read the Guardrails ([M4](M4-the-guardrails.md)). Anti-slop is always on. Cyber risk is two-speed. Learn where the demo-to-deploy line sits.
- [ ] Read the architecture map: the three tiers you build (Foundation, Workflow, Autonomous) and the five patterns. Learn to place a build on the tiers and to say why a client starts at Pattern 1 or 2. The Workflow and Autonomous tiers are the academy's stated frontier, named but not yet taught. (the curriculum, plus `02. Marketing and Propositions/Frameworks/agentic-patterns.html`)
- [ ] Tour the Hub ([M5](M5-the-hub.md)). The skills, the five founder voices, the wiki, the templates.
- [ ] Learn the brain. How we build a client's context layer ([M8](M8-context-layer-leave-behind.md)) with `/context-layer` and `/improve-brain-architecture`, and the security standard that governs it (`.claude/standards/BRAIN-SECURITY.md`): one brain per client, nothing crosses, strip before handover. This is core to how we work and what we leave behind. Two ideas to hold: a brain's value comes from (1) curation, the human judgement of what matters, at what fidelity, and what to trust, and (2) intelligent pathing, the links, spines and aggregation layers an agent travels. The second is largely automated, since the note graph is the source of truth and every index, spine and rollup is a cheap generated view over it, so we add pathing freely and spend our judgement on curation. The method lives in `/improve-brain-architecture`.
- [ ] Shadow one live demo or deliverable. Watch the flywheel run for real.
- [ ] Make one thing yourself:
  - If you build: an SLC single-page tool on synthetic data. Run `/impeccable` on the frontend, then `/critique`. Ship it to a link.
  - If you do not build: write a golden-question set for a real business unit, or run a devil's-advocate pass on a live deliverable. These are the parts of a build that decide whether it works.

By end of week one: you have run the discipline, read the method, and made one real thing.

## Month 1: contribute on real work, earn your first badge

- [ ] Join the cadence: fortnightly Lunch & Learn, monthly Deep Dive, the quarterly Capstone. Bring real work to each. (`04. HR and Training/04. Training Program/`)
- [ ] Work the Knowledge Builder track to your tier:
  - **Tier 0, Knowledge Partner:** run the agentic-readiness diagnostic, lead an ontology canvas, write a golden-question set, run data triage and adoption.
  - **Tier 1, Builder:** stand up the retrieval layers on a real stack, run the eval.
  - **Tier 2, Architect:** apprentice on architecture, pattern selection and the permissions trust boundary.
- [ ] Earn at least the Knowledge Partner badge: facilitate one ontology canvas and write one golden-question set that passes Architect review, on a real or practice business unit. Build 0 (the dogfood build on our own hub) is where the first cohort earns theirs.

By end of month one: you have contributed on a live POC at your tier and earned at least your Knowledge Partner badge.

## The ongoing rhythm

It does not add a meeting. It fills the slots the team already runs.

- **Fortnightly Lunch & Learn** (Tue, 45 min): the Tier 0 and lighter Tier 1 modules. Light prep.
- **Monthly Deep Dive** (last Fri, 2 hr): the hands-on Tier 1 modules and the ontology workshop. Bring a real business unit.
- **Quarterly Capstone** (half-day): run a knowledge-layer POC on a real-shaped brief.

## What we ask of you

- **Show up.** 75% or better attendance. Recordings posted otherwise.
- **Reach your tier.** Every Partner earns at least the Knowledge Partner badge. Builders and Architects are a smaller, named group.
- **Bring real work.** One real question or data sample per Deep Dive.
- **Lead once.** Each senior Partner leads or co-leads one module in their strength.

## Who teaches what

Toni and JC on building and prototyping. Rene on ontology and elicitation. Caitlin on questions, QA and adoption. Kim on architecture, permissions, pattern selection and build-versus-buy. Toni and Kim on agentic readiness.

## The one mindset

Ask the dumb questions, on purpose. You do not need to be technical or creative to lead this work. Tell the machine to explain it to a non-techie, then tell it to do the thing. The machine handles the parts that used to need a technical team. Your judgement is the value.
